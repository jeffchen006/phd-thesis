\subsection{Thesis Positioning}

This thesis contributes more practical defense layers for
\emph{high-stakes software systems under untrusted development and use}.

More specifically, the dissertation studies two families of 
increasingly autonomous software systems:
smart contracts that execute high-stakes financial 
logic under open on-chain interaction,
and LLM-based coding systems that translate 
vague human requests into code and system actions 
while relying on opaque models and weakly trusted information sources.
In both families, harmful behavior may be induced by factors 
that cannot be fully enumerated or predicted in advance,
including adversarial user actions, unexpected execution patterns,
contaminated training data, and poisoned external content.

This is the central positioning of the dissertation:
smart contracts and coding agents are two important instances 
of high-stakes software systems
that increasingly act with autonomy under development and use
that cannot be fully trusted.
Viewed this way, the key question is not only what 
each prior line of work solves in isolation,
but also where in the lifecycle of software behavior 
a defense intervenes:
at execution time, during system interaction, 
or earlier in the information pipeline that 
shapes system behavior.
The next section makes this layered defense perspective explicit
and uses it to organize the four works of the dissertation.
