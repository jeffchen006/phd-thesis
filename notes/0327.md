# Response to Committee Comments

## 1. Section 1.1

> We do not need Section 1.1.

 I removed the original preamble and replaced it with a Motivation section.

## 2. The shared umbrella

> The shared umbrella is not good enough. It reads like parallel stories forced into one.

In Section 1.1, I present the development lifecycle of high-stakes software
and map my completed work onto its stages: development, auditing, deployment, and patching.
The goal of this thesis is to protect the stakes held within high-stakes software, since
each of these stages can introduce risk.

## 3. Ties to blockchain

> When we talk about emerging software, it is obviously tied to the blockchain side. There
> is a series of blockchain-poisoning incidents that can serve as part of the motivation.

I use three motivating examples in Section 1.2 to further motivate the work:

- Example 1: AI-assisted development introduces malicious code during code generation,
  causing financial loss.
- Example 2: a domain-specific, security-oriented AI review fails to catch a bug,
  again causing financial loss.
- Example 3: even software developed by humans and reviewed by human experts can still
  incur financial loss.

Although all three remain blockchain-related, I emphasize only that the stakes lost are
cryptocurrencies, since they are the most obvious and direct form of high-stakes loss
managed by software. The same concepts also apply to other stakes, such as credentials and
biometric data; those are simply harder to measure.

## 4. Too much terminology

> There are too many terminologies in the first chapters, which makes them annoying to read.
> Imagine the audience as not only the committee but also the public: one goal of a thesis
> is to enable outsiders. There is one step of difference between the paper audience and the
> thesis audience.

 I removed all formal definitions from Section 1. In Chapter 1, I informally
introduce what high-stakes systems, untrusted development, and untrusted use are. I moved
the formal definitions and preliminaries for LLMs to Chapter 2, and those for smart
contracts and related blockchain concepts to Chapter 3.

## 5. Motivation vs. scope

> The motivation does not need to align with the scope. It should be more self-contained.

Chapter 1 focuses on the development workflow of high-stakes software systems,
as presented in Figure 1.1, and on all the risks introduced during development and
deployment. I want to explicitly distinguish high-stakes software systems, which require
substantial protection, from one-off, disposable code that can be cheaply generated by LLMs
(so-called "cheap code").
